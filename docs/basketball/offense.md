# Offense

The vocabulary, actions, and systems of NBA offense, and where each shows
up in the lake. Read [README.md](README.md) first.

## Floor and possession vocabulary

- **Spots.** Top of the key, slots (between top and wing), wings, corners,
  elbows (corners of the free throw line), blocks (beside the lane), short
  corner (baseline inside the arc), dunker spot (baseline just outside the
  lane), the nail (middle of the free throw line), the restricted area arc
  4 feet from the rim.
- **Sides.** Strong or ball side holds the ball. Weak or help side is the
  other. Swinging the ball moves it side to side to make the defense shift.
- **Phases.** Transition (before the defense sets), early or secondary
  offense (flowing into an action before the defense is matched up), half
  court, and late clock. After timeout plays (ATOs) and inbounds plays
  (baseline BLOB, sideline SLOB) are designed sets.
- **Advantage words.** Paint touch, drive and kick, closeout (a defender
  running at a shooter), attacking a closeout, extra pass, skip pass
  (over the defense to the far side), hockey assist (the pass before the
  assist), 2-for-1 (a quick shot late in a quarter to get the last
  possession too).

## Actions

### Ball screens

The pick and roll: a screener sets a pick on the ball handler's defender.
The handler can pull up, drive, split the two defenders, reject the screen,
or pass to the screener. The screener can:

- **Roll** to the rim, a lob threat that pulls the help defender in.
- **Pop** to the arc or midrange, a stretch big who pulls his defender out.
- **Short roll** into the space between the arc and the paint, catching a
  pocket pass against a blitz and playing four on three (Draymond Green).
- **Slip** early before contact, beating a defense that switches or
  hedges early. A **ghost** screen fakes the screen entirely.

Variants: high (top of the key), side, step-up (from the wing), drag (in
transition), double drag (two screeners), empty side (no defender on that
side to help), Spain (a third player back-screens the roll man's defender),
re-screen, and Horns (two bigs at the elbows, the handler picks a side).

### Handoffs and off-ball screens

- **Dribble handoff (DHO).** A big hands the ball to a cutter and screens
  his defender in one motion. **Zoom** or Chicago action is a pin-down
  into a handoff.
- **Down screen or pin-down.** A screen toward the baseline for a shooter
  coming up to the wing. The shooter reads his defender: curl to the rim,
  fade away, or pop straight out.
- **Flare.** A screen that pushes the shooter away from the ball.
- **Back screen.** A screen behind a defender so the cutter goes to the
  rim. **UCLA** is a back screen at the high post for the guard who just
  passed to the wing.
- **Cross screen.** Across the lane, usually for a post player.
- **Stagger and elevator.** Two screens in a row, or two screeners
  closing like doors after the shooter passes between them.
- **Floppy.** A shooter starts under the basket and chooses a side off
  single and double screens.
- **Screen the screener (STS) and Ram.** A screen for the player about to
  set a screen, which frees him or occupies his defender. Ram precedes a
  pick and roll.
- **Hammer.** A baseline drive while a weak side screen frees a shooter in
  the far corner (the Spurs).
- **Split action.** Two players screen for each other above a post touch,
  the core of the Warriors' motion and of the triangle.
- **Iverson cut.** A wing runs across the top off two screens to catch
  above the arc.

### Cuts, posts, isolations

- **Cuts.** Backdoor (behind an overplaying defender), basket cut, 45 cut,
  dive. Cuts are the most efficient play type because they finish at the
  rim, and they depend on someone drawing attention.
- **Post-up.** A seal on the block, entry pass, then a move, a face-up, or
  a kick-out against a double team. High-low passes go from the elbow to
  the block. Today the post is mostly a mismatch play after a switch or a
  hub for passing (Nikola Jokic).
- **Isolation.** The floor is cleared for one player against one defender.
  Often preceded by a screen to force a switch onto a weak defender,
  called switch or mismatch hunting.

### Transition

Fill the lanes (ball in the middle, wings wide, a rim runner), attack
before the defense matches up, trail into a drag screen if the break
stalls. Transition is the most efficient high-volume play type and is fed by
live-ball turnovers and long rebounds. Crashing the offensive glass
trades transition defense for second chances. League offensive rebound
rate fell from about 33 percent in 1979-80 to 22.5 in 2019-20 and rose to
26 in 2025-26 (lake).

## Systems

| System | Who, when | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| Post-centric inside-out | Mikan, Chamberlain, Abdul-Jabbar, Olajuwon, O'Neal | Feed the big, punish doubles with kick-outs |
| Fast break | Celtics of the 1960s, Showtime Lakers (Magic Johnson, 1980s) | Rebound, outlet, run |
| Motion and passing game | Bob Knight's motion, many college programs | Rules, not plays: screen, cut, and space by reads |
| Triangle | Tex Winter, Phil Jackson's Bulls and Lakers, 11 titles | Sideline triangle, post hub, every pass has counters |
| Princeton | Pete Carril. Kings and Nets elements in the 2000s | Backdoor cuts and a passing big at the high post |
| Isolation and post era | 1990s Knicks, Heat, Rockets | Slow pace, hand-checking, illegal defense rules kept help away |
| Seven Seconds or Less | Mike D'Antoni and Steve Nash's Suns, 2004-05 to 2007-08 | Early spread pick and roll, small ball, shoot early |
| Ball movement | Spurs, 2013-14 title | Shoot, pass, or drive within about half a second of the catch |
| Motion with a shooting star | Steve Kerr's Warriors, 2014-15 on | Split cuts, off-ball screens for Stephen Curry, Green's short roll |
| Heliocentric | Harden's Rockets, Luka Doncic | One creator, four spacers, iso and pick and roll |
| Moreyball | Daryl Morey's Rockets, 2017-18 peak | Only rim, threes, and free throws |
| Five out and center hub | Nuggets with Jokic, many 2020s teams | Every player spaces, a passing big runs handoffs and cuts |

The trend over 60 years runs one way: from the post toward the perimeter,
from the long two toward the three, and from set plays toward reads.
Long twos fell from 26.5 percent of attempts in 2011-12 to 8.2 percent in
2025-26 (lake).

## Offensive roles

| Role | Signature in the data |
|---|---|
| Primary creator | High USG% and AST%, many drives and pull-ups, high time of possession |
| Secondary creator | Moderate usage, attacks closeouts, handoffs |
| Spot-up shooter | High 3PAr, mostly assisted, catch-and-shoot share |
| Connector | Low usage, quick decisions, assists and secondary assists, few turnovers |
| Roll man and lob threat | Rim attempts, PRRollMan and Cut play types, screen assists |
| Stretch big | Pop threes, pulls the rim protector away |
| Post hub | Elbow and post touches, passes from the post |
| Offensive rebounder | ORB%, putbacks |

**Gravity** is how much a player's presence bends the defense. It shows up
indirectly in teammates' shot quality and efficiency with him on the
floor, not in his own line.

## Play types in the lake

`nba.player_season_play_types` from 2015-16, Synergy's classification of
the action that ended each possession. 2025-26 regular season, offense,
summed over players (lake):

| Play type | Share | PPP | Turnover share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spotup | 24.1 | 1.05 | 5.0 |
| Transition | 18.4 | 1.14 | 12.1 |
| PRBallHandler | 16.3 | 0.88 | 15.8 |
| Isolation | 7.5 | 0.93 | 10.2 |
| Cut | 6.7 | 1.32 | 5.1 |
| PRRollMan | 5.2 | 1.13 | 6.8 |
| OffRebound (putbacks) | 5.1 | 1.13 | 7.5 |
| Misc | 5.1 | 0.53 | 59.1 |
| Handoff | 4.6 | 0.95 | 11.1 |
| OffScreen | 3.7 | 0.99 | 7.3 |
| Postup | 3.4 | 1.02 | 12.1 |

How to read it: points per possession measures the finisher of the play,
not the value of the action. A pick and roll that draws help and ends in a
spot-up three is credited to Spotup. Isolation and ball-handler numbers
look poor partly because they absorb late-clock bailout possessions. Misc
is mostly turnovers no other type claims. A percentile compares the player
with others in the same play type.

Other offense data: `nba.pbp_qualifiers` flags fast break, second chance,
points off turnovers, and paint points from 2020-21 to 2025-26.
`nba.player_season_tracking` has drives, touches, passing, and pull-up and
catch-and-shoot splits from 2013-14. `shot_zones(player_id, season)` gives a
player's shot map against the league.
